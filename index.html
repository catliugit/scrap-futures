The problem

Recyclers can’t scale on scraps they can’t see coming.

Cutting-room waste is planned, in effect, the moment a marker is made. Yet today it is only counted after the run, sorted inconsistently, and sold off as low-grade bales.

Recyclers

No forward supply, no finance

Fibre-to-fibre plants need contracted, spec-matched feedstock to raise capital and fill capacity. They get unpredictable lots after the fact.

Factories

Offcuts as a cost, not inventory

Fibre, colour and run data exist in the cutting room, but aren’t captured in a form a buyer can contract against.

Brands

Liability without visibility

EPR and reporting rules make brands answerable for supply-chain waste they can neither forecast nor prove the destination of.

Why now

Regulation is turning waste into a reportable asset.

Three shifts are making pre-consumer offcuts something brands have to measure, and recyclers have to secure.

2025

Textile EPR in the EU

The revised Waste Framework Directive makes producers financially responsible for textile waste, with fees modulated by circularity.

2026

No more destroying stock

ESPR bans large companies from destroying unsold apparel and footwear, pushing waste accountability upstream.

Now

Recyclers need offtake

New fibre-to-fibre capacity is being built across Europe and South Asia and needs contracted, graded inputs to be bankable.

The product

From cutting plan to contracted feedstock.

Software plus coordination. Material ships directly from factory to recycler; Scrap Futures never holds stock.

01

Read the run

Ingest production orders, fabric specs and marker (cutting-layout) files from the factory.

02

Forecast the offcut

Predict volume and composition per run, then calibrate against actual weighed output.

03

Grade to a standard

Classify lots by fibre, colour and quality against recycler acceptance specs.

04

Match, ship, verify

Pre-match lots to recyclers before cutting, then record weights and shipment evidence for brand reporting.

Business model

Brands pay for visibility. Every lot pays a match fee.

SaaS revenue from the brand’s factory programme, plus a brokerage fee on verified, completed material transactions.

Programme setup£1k–£4k

One-off: map a brand’s factory network, connect data, set grade specs.

Platform£300–£900

Per month, per brand programme: forecasts, grading and reporting.

Brokerage5–12%
