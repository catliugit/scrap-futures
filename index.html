The rule

Destroying unsold clothes is now banned.

The EU’s Ecodesign Regulation bans large companies from destroying unsold apparel, accessories and footwear, and “destroying” includes shredding for recycling. UK and other non-EU brands selling into the EU are covered too.

2024

Disclose

Large companies publish, every year, what unsold stock they discarded and why.

Jul 2026

Ban in force

No destroying unsold clothes or shoes, except under 10 narrow exceptions, each needing evidence.

Mar 2027

Standard table

A fixed disclosure format, checked against your records to within 10%.

2030

Medium firms join

The ban extends to medium-sized companies: several times more brands in scope.

§

Example of the paperwork: before unsold goods can be destroyed under the donation exception, they must be offered for at least 8 weeks to at least 3 social-economy organisations. Records are kept for 5 years and produced within 30 days of a request. Sources: Regulation (EU) 2024/1781; Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/296; Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/2.

How the model works, and its limits
  • Data: 8,000 production runs from a simulator that nests graded pattern pieces into cutting plans, then adds the losses cutting plans miss: selvedge trim, ply-end allowance, roll-end remnants, spreading loss and defects.
  • Model: gradient-boosted quantile regression (P10/P50/P90) on order data only, exported to JSON and run here in plain JavaScript.
  • Held-out accuracy: 8.8% mean error on offcut kg (82% of runs within ±15%), vs 23.3% for the cutting plan alone and 20.3% for a flat 15% rule. The P10–P90 range covers 76% of outcomes. Simulated waste averages 19% of fabric, inside the 10–20% industry range.
  • Limits: trained on simulated, not factory, data. The first pilot goal is ±15% on real weighed output. Grades follow a v0 standard to be co-defined with recyclers.

Why I’m building this

Over the past five years, it feels like we’ve been buying more than ever: more clothes, more deliveries, more things worn a few times and then forgotten. What bothers me most is how normal it’s become. Perfectly good clothes get shredded or burned because that’s cheaper than finding them a home, and most of us never hear about it.

I don’t think the answer is making people feel guilty. It’s making the waste visible, and making the better option the easy one. That’s what Scrap Futures is for: helping brands find a home for every unsold piece, and, over time, make less of what nobody buys. I want to use what I’m good at to help us waste less of what we already have.

My background

I’m a data analyst from California, now based in London, with seven years of experience across aviation, banking and healthcare. At Bank of Hawaii I built treasury forecasts and automated financial reporting, cutting manual reporting work by more than half. At Hawaiian Airlines I ran workforce analytics and built the dashboards HR and leadership used for payroll and benefits decisions. Along the way I started my own creative marketing business, working with small businesses across Honolulu.

I recently finished an MSc in AI & Data Analytics at Northeastern University London, where my dissertation tested whether 10 AI models ask the right questions before giving financial advice. Most of my work comes down to the same thing: taking messy data and turning it into something people can act on. Waste is a data problem too. You can’t fix what you can’t see.

FAQ

Common questions.

Something not covered? Ask in the form below.

Does the ban apply to UK brands?

Yes, if they place clothing or footwear on the EU market and count as a large company (250+ staff, or over €50m turnover and €43m balance sheet, including linked companies). Medium-sized companies are covered from July 2030. This is general information, not legal advice.

Can’t we just recycle unsold stock?

Not freely. Under the EU rules, recycling unsold products counts as destruction. It’s allowed only under one of ten exceptions, with evidence, and after better options such as reuse.

Is the product live?

Not yet. We’re building it with a small group of design partners. Partners shape the product and get early pricing.

What does it cost?

Brands pay a yearly subscription plus a small share of the extra money recovered on routed stock. Outlets, charities and reprocessors join free. Design-partner terms are agreed one-to-one.

Do you buy or store stock?

No. Stock moves directly from the brand’s warehouse to the outlet. We provide the routing, the records and the reporting.
