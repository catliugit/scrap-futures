The problem

Recyclers can’t scale on scraps they can’t see coming.

Cutting-room waste is effectively decided when the cutting plan is made. Yet it’s only counted after the run, sorted inconsistently, and sold off as low-grade bales. Textile Exchange’s October 2026 analysis names brand purchase commitments and common feedstock grades as the fastest route to recycler finance (Resource Recycling).

Liability without visibility

EPR and reporting rules make you answerable for supply-chain waste you can neither forecast nor prove the destination of.

Method and limits of this prototype
  • Mass = fabric length × width × GSM; offcuts = mass × (1 − cutting utilisation).
  • The ±20% band is a planning assumption, not a measured error. The pilot goal is a forecast within ±15% of weighed actuals.
  • Grades and routes are rule-based placeholders. The real grade standard will be co-defined with recyclers.
  • The production model will learn from marker files and weighed output across runs.

How the model works (assumptions)
  • 10,000 Monte Carlo runs × 24 months, fixed seed, with triangular (min / most likely / max) distributions for prices, sales pace and hours per client.
  • Founder churn is included: a monthly chance of quitting that falls as fit with the founder rises.
  • Compared against six alternative venture ideas using the same engine; Scrap Futures was the only one high on both founder fit and payoff.
  • The stress test lowers prices and slows sales. The biggest untested input is brand willingness to pay.

FAQ

Common questions.

Something not covered? Use the form below.

How is this different from waste-traceability platforms?

Traceability platforms such as Reverse Resources map and trace factory waste after it’s generated. Scrap Futures starts earlier: it forecasts offcuts from the cutting plan before the cut, so lots can be graded and matched to a recycler in advance.

Do you buy, store or handle the material?

No. Material ships directly from the factory to the recycler. We provide the forecast, the grading, the match and the evidence trail.

What data do you need from a factory?

Production orders, fabric specifications (composition, width, GSM) and marker or cutting-layout files where available. Weighed offcut output after each run lets us calibrate the forecast.

Who defines the grades?

Grades are co-defined with recyclers against their acceptance specs, so one lot description works across several buyers. Founding pilot partners help shape version 1.

What does a founding pilot involve?

One brand, one or two factories, and a few production runs. We forecast each run, compare it with weighed actuals, and work towards a first recycler match. Scope and any fee are agreed up front.

Why London?

London sits close to UK and EU brands facing EPR and ESPR, and to Europe’s recycling and climate-investment ecosystem. The factories are in sourcing hubs such as Bangladesh, Pakistan, Portugal and Turkey.
