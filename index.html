The problem

Recyclers can’t scale on scraps they can’t see coming.

Cutting-room waste is effectively decided when the cutting plan is made. Yet it’s only weighed after the run, sorted inconsistently, and mostly exported or sold as low-grade bales. Recyclers say they can’t build plants without committed, predictable supply (Kearney 2026; Textile Exchange).

Today

Liability without visibility

Recycled-content targets and upcoming EU rules on waste-origin disclosure need evidence you can’t get from a waste bale.

How the model works, and its limits
  • Data: 8,000 production runs from a simulator that nests graded pattern pieces into cutting plans, then adds the losses cutting plans miss: selvedge trim, ply-end allowance, roll-end remnants, spreading loss and defects.
  • Model: gradient-boosted quantile regression (P10/P50/P90) on order data only, exported to JSON and run here in plain JavaScript.
  • Held-out accuracy: 8.8% mean error on offcut kg (82% of runs within ±15%), vs 23.3% for the cutting plan alone and 20.3% for a flat 15% rule. The P10–P90 range covers 76% of outcomes. Simulated waste averages 19% of fabric, inside the 10–20% industry range.
  • Limits: trained on simulated, not factory, data. The first pilot goal is ±15% on real weighed output. Grades follow a v0 standard to be co-defined with recyclers.

FAQ

Common questions.

Something not covered? Use the form below.

Doesn’t cutting software already know the waste?

It knows the planned waste inside one marker. It doesn’t predict the weighed offcut after ends, remnants, spreading and defects (about a quarter more in our model), split by recyclable grade, early enough to sign a forward contract. We aim to integrate with cutting software, not replace it.

How is this different from waste-traceability platforms?

Traceability platforms such as Reverse Resources map and trace factory waste after it’s generated. Scrap Futures starts earlier: it forecasts offcuts from the cutting plan before the cut, so lots can be graded and matched to a recycler in advance.

Do you buy, store or handle the material?

No. Material ships directly from the factory to the recycler. We provide the forecast, the grading, the match and the evidence trail.

What data do you need from a factory?

Production orders, fabric specifications (composition, width, GSM) and marker or cutting-layout files where available. Weighed offcut output after each run lets us calibrate the forecast.

Who defines the grades?

We build on Textile Exchange’s Recycled Material Data Form (fibre composition, colour, origin and source) and map it to each recycler’s acceptance specs, rather than inventing a parallel standard.

What does a founding pilot involve?

One brand, one or two factories, and a few production runs. We forecast each run, compare it with weighed actuals, and work towards a first recycler match. A founding pilot is a fixed-fee engagement agreed up front.

Why London?

London sits close to UK and EU brands facing EPR and ESPR, and to Europe’s recycling and climate-investment ecosystem. The factories are in sourcing hubs such as Bangladesh, Pakistan, Portugal and Turkey.
